How to use your planner

Three steps take you from purchase to a printed planner:

  1. Get your code. Your access code arrives with your purchase delivery. Enter your name and the code on the lock screen to unlock the tool — it stays unlocked on this device.

Get ready for Ramadan — prep checklist

A few minutes of preparation before Day 1 makes the whole month easier:

  • Set the start date for your country — Ramadan begins with the sighting of the moon, so check your local date (the planner default is 8 February 2027).
  • Choose your language and paper size before you generate.
  • Print single-sided, or double-sided to save paper and ink — the Ink-saver option lightens the cover and colours too.
  • Bind, staple or folder the pages so nothing gets lost.
  • Write the Suhoor and Iftar times for week 1 into the daily pages.
  • Write your name on the cover — and your child’s name on their certificate.
  • Tick any extras you want before printing, so everything comes out in one go.

Learn the duas

Your planner carries two dua collections — every dua is quoted exactly as it is transmitted, with its source printed next to it:

  • 30 daily duas — one on every daily page, in Arabic with the meaning in your chosen language. Read the day’s dua at Suhoor or Iftar and try to learn it by heart during the day.
  • 52 duas from the Qur’an — an optional extra: the “Rabbana” duas and the prayers of the prophets and the believers, each with its surah and verse.
  • Printable dua cards — another optional extra: all 30 daily duas as cut-out cards (5 pages, 6 cards per page). Cut along the dashed lines and keep a card where you will see it — on the fridge, in your Qur’an, by the door.

A simple way to learn them: one dua a day. Read it in the morning, repeat it through the day, and by Eid you will know all 30.

Quran reading plan

The Qur’an is divided into 30 parts, called juz. Read one juz a day and you complete the whole Qur’an inside Ramadan — a khatma.

  • The Qur’an Khatma Tracker (page 3 of every planner) pairs each day with its juz — tick each juz as you finish it.
  • Milestones: end of week 1 → juz 7, week 2 → juz 14, week 3 → juz 21, week 4 → juz 28, and day 30 → juz 30.
  • Want to track by surah instead? Add the Qur’an Reading Tracker extra — all 114 surahs in order, each with its verse count, a tick box and a date line.
  • Missed a day? Read a little extra the next day and keep going — a khatma finished late is still a khatma.

Ramadan with kids

The Kids’ Ramadan Pack (included free — keep its tick box on when you generate) turns Ramadan into something children can see, touch and count:

  • 30-day star chart — your child colours a star every day they fast (even a little fast), pray, do a good deed or read Qur’an.
  • 30 good-deed cards — one simple deed per day: read the card together, do the deed, colour the star on the card.
  • Up to 50 colouring pages — you choose how many go into the book (6 in the standard book): lanterns, mosques and other Ramadan objects (pictures of objects only).
  • “My First Ramadan” certificate — printed with your child’s name, ready to present on Eid day.

Tip: print the kids’ pages on slightly thicker paper if you can — the star chart and certificate survive the month much better.
